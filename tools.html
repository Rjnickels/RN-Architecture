Extension tools

Two free checks for small single-storey extensions to houses in England: U-value targets and the glazing limit.

U-value targets

Maximum U-values for new walls, roofs, floors and openings in an extension to an existing house in England.

ElementMaximum U-value (W/m²K)
Walls0.18
Roof0.15
Floor0.18
Windows1.4, or Window Energy Rating Band B minimum
Doors1.4
Rooflights2.2

From Approved Document L Volume 1 (2021 edition with 2023 amendments), Table 4.2. A 2026 edition has been published (Table 3.2), so check which edition applies on your application date. For a ground floor, the U-value also depends on its exposed perimeter and area, and for an extension it can be worked out using the perimeter and area of the whole enlarged house. The glazing limit below can force lower values if you go over it.

Working out how much insulation you need

Glazing limit check

The windows, doors and rooflights in an extension shouldn’t add up to more than 25% of its floor area, plus the area of any existing windows or doors the extension covers over or removes.

New windows, doors and rooflights in the extension (every external door counts)
Existing windows or doors the extension covers or removes (optional)

Enter the floor area and your openings.

Based on Approved Document L Volume 1 (England), the extension glazing rule. Measure the overall size of each opening including the frame. If you’re close to the limit, ask your building control officer how they measure. If you’re over, building control will want a heat-loss calculation (an area-weighted U-value comparison, or SAP) to show the extension still complies. Check it against the edition that applies to your job.
